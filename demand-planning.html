The problem

Retail planners need a realistic view of next-day unit sales. A complex model is useful only if it improves on a simple rule using information actually available at forecast time.

The decision: Keep the best validated method per product; review large misses.

What I built

A chronological backtest for five frequently ordered products, validation-based model selection, and a planning scenario calculator.

What the evidence says

13.9% lower average error than the weekly baseline

The validation-selected methods achieved 75.5 units mean absolute error versus 87.7 on 150 held-out SKU-days.

Machine learning selected for 2 of 5 products

October validation chose a method per product. The remaining products kept last week’s sales as their forecast. November was held out until evaluation.

The forecast is still too uncertain to automate replenishment

Weighted absolute percentage error is 86.6%. Investigate bulk-order spikes and test additional periods before using these forecasts operationally.

How it works

  1. Select the five most frequently ordered UK products using data before October only.
  2. Create a daily unit-sales series through November. Days without recorded sales are zero; no stock-availability data is available.
  3. Build lagged values at 1, 7, 14 and 28 days, shifted rolling means, and known calendar features. Every feature precedes its target.
  4. Train gradient boosting through September; compare it with a 7-day seasonal baseline on October MAE.
  5. Freeze the chosen method, refit using data through October, and evaluate one-day-ahead predictions in November. Earlier actuals may be used as each day arrives; this is not a 30-day-ahead forecast.

Limits & next steps

These are observed sales, not unconstrained demand. Stockouts, price changes, promotions and wholesale orders are not modeled. Only five products and one test month are evaluated; selection by order frequency does not represent the full catalog.

The all-product gradient-boosting result is also reported in the repository. It performs better on November, but choosing it after seeing that holdout would invalidate the selection process. The dashboard keeps the method chosen on October validation.

Next: rolling-origin evaluation across seasons, prediction intervals, inventory data, and a monitored planner pilot. Source: Chen (2015), UCI Online Retail, CC BY 4.0. No inventory savings or stockout reduction is claimed.
