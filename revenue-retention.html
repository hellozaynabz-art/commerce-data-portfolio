The problem

A retail team needs one consistent view of revenue and customer behavior. Gross sales alone hide credits, while anonymous purchases make customer metrics incomplete.

The decision: Prioritize lifecycle experiments and investigate credit patterns.

What I built

A SQL-backed revenue dashboard, observed customer cohorts, behavioral segments, and a transparent data-quality audit.

What the evidence says

£8,558,035 in net merchandise sales

£8,841,680 in positive sales less £283,645 in credit lines across complete January–November months.

62.6% observed repeat-buyer share

4,168 identified customers; only 86.5% of gross sales can be linked to a customer ID. Anonymous purchases are retained in revenue reporting.

A lifecycle experiment, with a clear denominator

Use active and lapsed repeat-buyer segments to design different outreach tests. Measure incremental net revenue and unsubscribe rates in a randomized experiment; no retention uplift is claimed here.

How it works

  1. Load the official UCI workbook and verify its archive checksum. Audit missing customer IDs and remove exact duplicate invoice lines.
  2. Keep product-like stock codes, nonzero quantities, and positive unit prices. Service and manual adjustment codes are excluded. Credits remain signed; positive non-cancelled lines form the purchase population.
  3. Restrict reporting to complete Jan–Nov 2011 months. Calculate monthly sales in SQL and reconcile the totals against Python.
  4. Use identified positive-sale customers for order frequency, recency segments, and cohorts. Preserve anonymous sales in the revenue denominator.

Limits & next steps

Revenue is merchandise value, not profit. Credit notes are not reliably matched to their original sale, so credit value divided by gross sales is not a product-return rate. Exact-duplicate removal assumes repeated invoice lines are accidental; legitimate repeated lines could be removed. Bulk orders and unusual quantities are retained and should be investigated.

The dataset is historical, covers one retailer, and includes wholesale behavior. Customer IDs are missing on many rows; first observed purchase is not necessarily the customer’s first purchase. A production version needs order identifiers, margin, attribution, and validated credit matching.

Source: Chen (2015), UCI Online Retail, CC BY 4.0. These are portfolio findings, not results from an employer engagement.
